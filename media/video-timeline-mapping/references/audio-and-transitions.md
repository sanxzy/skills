# Audio layers and transitions

Read alongside [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). The SRT owns narration timing and words; the timeline plans supporting sound and how visual/audio states connect. No sound asset is promised. Do not transcribe narration into visual sequences.

## Independent audio planning

- **Narration** is the intelligibility reference, not a track to rewrite. In the blueprint, use only SRT cue numbers/ranges and relevant gap/pause/resume states; the SRT owns all words and exact cue timing. A gap means no *narration*, not full-mix silence. Do not retime or suppress a cue to make room for another voice. If the user authorizes a narration revision, require its updated timed source before finalizing the handoff; until then, mark it conditional rather than rewriting the SRT inside this skill.
- **SOURCE_AUDIO** is original sound attached to supplied footage/source, or a clearly conditional requirement for downstream footage; it is not a separately created bed/effect. It includes source speech and `NAT_SOUND` (recorded birds, waterfall, weather, crowd, machinery, etc.). `NAT_SOUND` is a subtype/role, not another layer. Give `SRC-AUD-###` its own time span, linked `VIS-SEQ-###`, intended known/unknown content, provenance status, role and states (MUTED, FADE_IN, PRIMARY, ACTIVE, BACKGROUND, DUCKED, FADE_OUT). Original sound can take focus in an existing SRT gap; otherwise keep it subordinate to narration or request both authorization and an updated timed narration source. Never claim exact recorded sound, sync, translation, license or clip existence without source evidence. If none is specified, one global `SOURCE_AUDIO: none specified` is enough. See [cross-layer-sequences.md](cross-layer-sequences.md).
- **Music/background audio** follows narrative energy and purpose across sections and meaning changes, not necessarily beat boundaries. State the intended mood, energy, relative level, progression, start/end, pauses/returns and relation to speech when useful. No music is valid, including an entire video. An `OPTIONAL` bed is a proposal, not evidence of a track. Avoid music that dictates an unsupported verdict (e.g. ominous music under a neutral allegation).
- **AMBIENT** is a separately mapped environmental bed, whether chosen to bridge shots or supplement a recorded environment; it is not the sound originally captured with a specific shot. Ambience describes environment and perspective. An exterior environment can bridge several shots; a location or perspective shift can justify a fade, prelap or replacement. Do not restart ambience on each visual. Do not label invented sounds as synchronized original-location audio.
- **SFX** is added editorial sound, not an impact already captured in footage. Specify its event, purpose, time (point or interval), relative intensity and relationship to speech. If captured wind, roof impact, bird calls or waterfall sound already delivers the effect, don't map a synthetic duplicate without a separate purpose. No whoosh on every cut. If no effects are needed, state one global absence policy instead of repeated `SFX: NONE` lines.
- **Intentional absence** may be NO_MUSIC, NO_AMBIENT, NO_SFX or FULL_SILENCE, but full silence also removes narration: never use it during spoken cues unless explicitly overriding the source with justification. Duck, reduce or omit a competing layer; absolute dB targets are not required for editorial planning.

SOURCE_AUDIO, music, ambience and SFX have their **own time intervals** even when visual sequences or narrative beats change. Record them once in the Audio Timeline. A sequence's audio note should refer to those events or only describe a meaningful change, not duplicate their full timings. Narration cue ranges are included for alignment, not as a second transcript. In an audio-only gap, keep a coherent visual state and specify whether a music marker is observed in the SRT or merely a proposed editorial bed. Check that any visual/music co-evolution remains a choice, not a claim about actual recordings.

## Sensory beat (editorial window, not another layer)

A `SENSORY_BEAT` proposes a continuing visual sequence and its original sound carrying the moment while narration pauses; it becomes executable only after that sound is verified. Specify linked `VIS-SEQ`/`SRC-AUD` IDs, an actual SRT speech gap or explicitly authorized revision with a revised timed source, source-audio PRIMARY window, return to BACKGROUND/DUCKED as narration resumes, and music/ambient/SFX interaction. It need not be a cut or another visual sequence. Use it only when seeing **and hearing** the subject adds value (natural scale, environmental presence, documented speech, audience response); don't manufacture sound to fill a gap. If the desired clip/audio is not provided, label the region **conditional/unresolved**. See the [cross-layer example](cross-layer-sequences.md) and the conditional [Giethoorn natural-sound window](../examples/bagaimana-hidup-ketika-kanal-menjadi-jalan-id.timeline.md).

## Choose the simplest transition that satisfies the relationship

First ask whether the visual sequence actually changes; if not, HOLD/CONTINUE even when the narrative beat or audio state changes. For a true boundary, consider subject, time, space, cause/effect, reveal order, look direction, visual similarity and which sounds should carry. A CUT is often sufficient. A HARD_CUT can emphasize a supported contrast. MATCH_CUT requires a stated meaningful match (shape, motion, composition, concept). DISSOLVE/CROSSFADE may communicate elapsed time or gradual transformation; not every change deserves a visible effect. GRAPHIC/MOTION_TRANSITION should have a clear explanatory or requested stylistic function.

A J-cut is **incoming scene audio before incoming scene visual**: name that audio, its start, the visual cut time and lead. An L-cut is **outgoing scene audio continuing after its visual leaves**: name the audio, end and overhang. A continuous voiceover across supporting images is simply continuing narration, *not* an L-cut on its own. For a `SOURCE_AUDIO` J/L cut, identify `SRC-AUD` and `VIS-SEQ` IDs, audio lead/overhang and the visual cut. For music/ambient bridges, identify which ongoing region crosses the visual cut; do not create a new music event or falsely claim sound is attached to a particular clip. A cutaway/insert specifies the returning view where important.

### Illustrative J-cut (not actual sourced media)

Current visual `VIS-SEQ-011` ends at `00:01:24.000`; incoming factory ambience `AMB-002` begins at `00:01:22.800`; factory visual `VIS-SEQ-012` begins at `00:01:24.000`. `TRN-004`: `J_CUT`, audio lead 1.200s. These times are only an illustrative diagram: do **not** insert them into a real plan without source/brief support. The ambient lead must be an editorial requirement, not a claim that a factory recording exists.

### Example of a non-J/L transition

Narration continues over a cut from a speaker to an explanatory graphic. No scene audio is prelapping or trailing. This is a CUT with continuous narration; do not name it L_CUT just because the spoken words continue. Conversely, an archival sequence may stay on screen while narration pauses and its original SOURCE_AUDIO becomes PRIMARY: that is an AUDIO_HANDOFF, not a picture cut or J/L cut.

## Independent timing and overlap review

For every bridge or effect, ensure start < end, times fall within the known/planned duration, the from/to sequences exist, the required audio region spans the intended boundary, and lead/overhang arithmetic is consistent. Any SOURCE_AUDIO-primary window that replaces narration must agree with the actual SRT speech gap; an authorized retime is conditional until a revised timed source exists. Narration can otherwise continue with source audio ducked. An independently mapped AMBIENT bed and SOURCE_AUDIO can coexist if their roles do not mask or duplicate each other. Check source sound is not both MUTED and PRIMARY at the same time. A dissolve entails overlapping visuals or a stated composite, not two conflicting full-screen requirements. An anticipatory sound/visual must not spoil a withheld answer. A motion accent should specify its target layer and bounded interval, with text/evidence remaining readable; don't shake the whole video merely to convey urgency.

Global review: repeated J-cuts, dramatic hits or fades can become as mechanical as cue-to-cut editing. Sustain the audio world where continuity matters, change it only when motivated, and retain silence/breathing room where it improves comprehension.
