# Pattern workshop

These are original craft exercises. Excerpts are intentionally short and are not complete default-duration deliverables. Hypothetical examples are marked; they must never be imported as researched events. Reverify factual material when adapting any example to a new script.

## 1. An opening that earns attention

Decorative draft:

> Above our heads, a hidden kingdom waits. A world of secrets. A world of power. But what happens when the sky can no longer hold its breath?

Problem: no clear subject or supported claim. The viewer must decode the imagery before learning anything.

Original mechanism-led alternative:

```text
[00:00.000–00:12.000] A cloud can hang above a town without bringing rain. Its water particles still need to grow before they can fall as precipitation.
```

Craft function: a concrete contrast identifies the topic and leads directly to the process. For use in a real script, verify this claim; the rain example's precipitation source demonstrates the evidence type.

## 2. A familiar question with an early answer

Hypothetical repair documentary; no real event asserted:

```text
[00:00.000–00:17.000] Imagine a village pump that stops working. The handle still moves, but no water reaches the spout. The first useful question is where the movement stops turning into water flow.

[00:18.000–00:33.000] In this example, the repair team finds a damaged seal. That gives the next part of the story a clear purpose: explain what the seal does and how the team tests the replacement.
```

This demonstrates task → obstacle → inspection → explanation. A real documentary must replace the hypothetical details with documented ones. Do not preserve an invented fault merely because it makes a tidy story.

## 3. Historical investigation without invented scenes

Hypothetical outline, not a sourced historical case:

| Beat | Narrative job | Evidence required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A later record contradicts an earlier account | Establish the problem | The actual records and what conflicts |
| Return to the earlier event | Orient chronology | Supported date, participants, location |
| Compare the evidence available then | Explain uncertainty | Contemporary documents and their limits |
| Show what the later record establishes | Advance the answer | Provenance, authenticity, relevant passage |
| State what remains unresolved | Honest resolution | Distinction between evidence and inference |

Weak reconstruction: “That cold night, he knew the investigation was closing in.”

Repair: describe the recorded action and sequence, then say what the evidence can establish. Weather, knowledge, and motive each need separate support. If the record does not establish motive, the script does not need one to explain the chronology.

## 4. Geographic movement with purpose

Hypothetical island script; all place details would require research:

Weak transition: “Next is another amazing village, full of beautiful houses and rich history.”

Better structural approach: move from an exposed harbor to the route carrying supplies inland, then to a settlement served by that route. Establish location, constraint, and consequence. The next place answers something the previous place made relevant.

Private beat outline:

| Beat | Picture opportunity | New understanding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Harbor and weather | Map and documented loading activity | Why delivery is constrained |
| Inland route | Route map and terrain | How supplies move |
| Local storage | Documented storage/commerce | How people manage intervals between deliveries |

Do not state the exact schedule, distances, or community behavior until supported. A scenic view alone cannot demonstrate a livelihood mechanism.

## 5. Comparison without a false leaderboard

Hypothetical choice between transport routes:

Weak framing: “Route A is clearly the best.”

Repair: define whether the question is speed, cost, reliability, access, or environmental impact. Apply the same measure to both routes. If the title asks a broader question, explain the tradeoffs instead of producing an unsupported combined score.

An observational result may justify “faster on this recorded trip.” It does not automatically justify “always faster” or “best for everyone.” Preserve the comparison's scope in narration.

## 6. Indonesian clarity without excessive slang

Original language exercise, illustrating a hypothetical water-flow comparison:

```text
[00:00.000–00:17.000] Bayangkan dua jalur air yang panjangnya sama. Jalur pertama lebar dan lurus. Jalur kedua sempit dan berbelok. Untuk membandingkannya, kita perlu tahu apa yang diukur: waktu perjalanan, jumlah air, atau biaya pembangunannya.

[00:18.000–00:33.000] Hasil untuk satu ukuran belum tentu menjawab ukuran yang lain. Karena itu, sebelum menyebut salah satu jalur lebih baik, kita harus menentukan tujuan perbandingannya.
```

The language is approachable without copying a creator's memes or catchphrases. The hypothetical framing avoids pretending the described routes were observed. Local speech rates and pauses still need a read-through.

## 7. Information density through causal order

Hypothetical production line:

Weak version: “The workshop has tools, storage, workers, a schedule, several rooms, and a long history.”

Better outline: establish what the workshop makes; explain one bottleneck; show the documented change; describe its measured consequence. Facts become a process the viewer can follow. Add history only if it explains the present arrangement.

## 8. Fixing repetition and multiple endings

Weak ending sequence:

> This shows how remarkable the process is. It teaches us an important lesson. It proves there is more than meets the eye. And perhaps that is the real story.

Repair: state the actual answer and its relevant implication once. Return to a concrete opening image if its meaning has changed. Do not add a second conclusion because the first sounded too plain.

## 9. Timing as an editorial decision

A 60-word explanation at 135 words per minute takes about 26.7 seconds before substantial pauses. Assigning it eight seconds forces implausible delivery. Assigning it a full minute conceals a sparse interval unless documented action actually carries that minute.

Repair the text or the budget. Cut redundant prose for a shorter requested video. For a longer one, research and explain another necessary mechanism, limitation, example, or consequence. Never repair insufficient content solely by moving the endpoint.
